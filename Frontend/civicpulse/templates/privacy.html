Data handling & DPDP Act 2023

How CivicPulse handles your data

A plain-language account of what this prototype collects, why, who can see it, and what's still an open question rather than a solved one — deliberately written for both citizens and evaluators to read the same page.

What's collected

An account: name, email, region, education, occupation, language, and (if you file as a government official) employee ID, department, and either a shared verification code or an ID document photo. A complaint: its title, body text, any before/after photos you attach, and the AI classification result (category, priority, confidence, risk flags).

DPDP Act 2023 — where this stands

India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 governs how personal data like this should be collected, used, and secured, including purpose limitation (only collect what a stated purpose needs), a right to correction/erasure, and breach-notification duties. This is a hackathon prototype, not a certified DPDP-compliant deployment — no formal Data Protection Officer, consent-manager integration, or breach-notice pipeline exists here yet. What the codebase does already do in that direction:

  • Passwords are hashed (werkzeug), never stored in plain text.
  • A citizen reporting corruption has their identity withheld from the accused department by default — the complaint routes to an independent vigilance channel instead (see the corruption-flag routing logic).
  • An ID document uploaded for officer verification is visible only to admin accounts reviewing that specific request, not to the general complaint queue.
  • An unverified allegation against a named individual is held for review and is never auto-attached to that person's official record from a single complaint.
Not yet built: field-level encryption at rest, a stated data-retention/deletion period, and a self-service "export or delete my data" flow. Named here rather than left unstated, since a hackathon evaluator asking about DPDP compliance deserves an honest "here's what exists, here's what doesn't" rather than silence.

Who can see what

You can see your own complaints and profile. Government officials (role-gated, see the account-type and officer-verification flows) can see the complaint queue for triage. Admin accounts can review pending officer verification requests, including any attached ID document. Nobody outside CivicPulse receives this data — there is no analytics/advertising pixel and no third-party data sale in this prototype.

External-system ingestion

CivicPulse also has a mock CPGRAMS-shaped ingestion bridge (disabled unless explicitly configured) for grievances filed through an external government system rather than this app directly. Grievances that arrive that way go through the exact same classification and handling described above.
